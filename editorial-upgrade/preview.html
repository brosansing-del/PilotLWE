Whose Liberation, After All!

Notes on bodily autonomy, marriage and reproductive choices within Indian Maoism.

N
The Naxal ArchivePublished 19 November 2026 · 4 min read

‘I was 29 when I underwent abortion. I was starting to have second thoughts about staying in the organization.. I wanted to keep the child, but that was not my decision to make.. now I am told that I will never be able to conceive..’ — Sameela, Narayanpur

One of the less discussed aspects of Indian Maoism is its treatment of marriage, reproduction and family life. The movement speaks of liberation from oppressive social structures, including patriarchy.

Yet within the underground organization, even the most intimate decisions—whom a cadre could marry, whether they could have children and when they could live with their spouse—could become matters of organizational control.

Marriage was permitted in some circumstances, but family life was treated as a potential threat to revolutionary commitment.
