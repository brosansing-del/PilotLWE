Naxalbari

The uprising, its agrarian roots and the events that shaped the early Naxalite movement.

Period
1967
Location
Naxalbari, West Bengal
Key issue
Agrarian unrest

Agrarian conditions and grievances

The Naxalbari uprising of 1967 emerged from longstanding agrarian tensions in the Naxalbari region of Darjeeling district in West Bengal. The conflict was rooted in the unequal distribution of land, the exploitation of sharecroppers and agricultural labourers, and the power exercised by jotedars and other landed interests over the rural population. Peasants faced insecure tenancy arrangements, arbitrary evictions, high rents and the denial of their rights over the land they cultivated.

Political and ideological background

The political developments within the Communist movement during the 1960s provided an important background to the uprising. The formation of the Communist Party of India (Marxist), or CPI(M), in November 1964 had followed a prolonged ideological conflict within the Communist Party of India over the character of the Indian state, the role of parliamentary politics and the path towards revolution.
